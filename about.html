About

"Not all those who wander are lost, but all who wander should have a good map."

The Tale of the Map

In the days when the world was young and the roads were many, travellers relied upon the lore of old and the guidance of friends. Now, in a later age, we have gathered the knowledge of many lands into a single chart: One Map to Rule Them All. Here the paths of Middle-earth and the realms beyond lie unrolled before you, from the green hills of the Shire to the shadowed gates of Mordor and Mount Doom.

Whether you seek the swiftest road to Rivendell or merely wish to wonder at how far the world stretches, this map shall serve as your faithful companion.

Our Fellowship

This work was fashioned by a small company of cartographers, scribes, and makers of code, bound not by a ring but by a shared love of maps and stories. Though our numbers be few, our purpose is true: to lay before every wanderer a map worthy of the journey.

Harry M. Foster

Scribe of Code

Harry M. Foster (He/Him) is a PhD candidate in the Department of English at Michigan State University. He completed his undergraduate degree in English and Creative Writing at Columbia University and served in for 5 years in the Marines. His research examines expressions and manifestations of cultural heroism across folklore, literature, film, and comics from the 19th century to the present. He is particularly interested in how heroic archetypes perform resistance and survival strategies while navigating systemic oppression. In addition to his academic work, Harry also publishes creative works as a companion to his academic work.

Gillian S. Macdonald

Scribe of the Code

Gillian S. Macdonald is the Director of the LEADR and the Computational Cultural Hertiage Fellowship and a core member of the faculty for both DH@MSU and the Center for European and Eurasian Studies at Michigan State University. She is a historian of early modern Scotland and the British Atlantic World and holds a PhD from Central Michigan University in Transnational and Comparative history. She has also studied at the University of Strathclyde in Glasgow, where she received her BA and MRes degrees.

The Lore We Draw Upon

Our charts are woven from the ancient craft of Leaflet, and the lands themselves are drawn from the open archives of OpenStreetMap contributors, who have toiled across many seasons to record the shape of the world. To them we owe a debt of gratitude.

A Word of Caution

Even the wise cannot see all ends. This map is offered as a guide, not a prophecy. Should you find a road that has changed, or a realm misplaced, bear no ill will, and send word so that we may mend it.
